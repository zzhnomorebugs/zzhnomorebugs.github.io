Property-driven protein design

01 Given a target property, generate sequences worth testing

Protein engineering seeks amino acid sequences with a desired function. Controllable generation gives the target property directly to the generative model, providing a clear direction for proposing candidates.

  1. Input: Reference sequence and target propertyThe reference sequence provides sequence context; the target condition describes the desired property.
  2. Generation: Stepwise conditional updatesGradually adjust amino acid class predictions under the constraints of a sequence prior.
  3. Output: Candidate variantsScreen candidates through independent evaluation for subsequent experimental validation.

A generative model must meet two requirements: the sequence itself should be plausible, and the generated result should respond to the target condition. When editing a reference sequence, the extent of the edits also needs to be controlled, so that pursuing the target property does not disrupt the original sequence and structure.

The project's central question

Can a general protein sequence prior provide a reusable foundation for different property tasks, with conditional adaptation producing candidates that move toward the desired target?

What does the target condition express?

A condition can describe a variant effect, a change in stability, or a binding property. The model learns the relationship between the target condition and sequence changes, while each task retains its own label definitions and evaluation methods.

What does the output mean?

The output consists of candidate sequences that require further validation. Predicted properties, confidence in predicted structures, and actual experimental effects provide distinct levels of evidence.

Dividing the work between a general prior and task conditions

02 Learn sequence patterns first, then learn target conditions

Natural protein sequences provide broad statistical patterns, a reference sequence supplies conditions for relative editing, and property labels specify the task's direction. These sources of information work together to generate candidates.

Unconditional pretraining first learns the relationship between noisy states and real protein sequences. In a downstream task, the model additionally receives property conditions and learns how to adjust its predictions within the same generative framework.

For editing tasks that start from a reference sequence, the objective can be expressed as learning a conditional distribution:

\[p_\theta(x\mid x_{\mathrm{ref}},y).\]

Here, \(x_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is the reference sequence, \(y\) is the target property, and \(x\) is a candidate sequence. This notation expresses the task objective; it does not imply that the model guarantees every candidate will satisfy the condition.

From conditional score decomposition to residual prediction

03 One model learns the sequence language; another learns to follow conditions

In conditional control for continuous diffusion, the conditional score can be decomposed into an unconditional score and a conditional correction. The former describes the sequence's own patterns; the latter gives the direction introduced by the target condition.

\[\underbrace{s_{\mathrm{c}}}_{\text{conditional score}} =\underbrace{s_{\mathrm{u}}}_{\text{unconditional score}} +\underbrace{\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(y\mid x_t)}_{\text{conditional correction}}.\]

This decomposition offers a way to understand conditional control in CFG and motivates the division of work between the project's two models:

General model: Learn the language of protein sequences

Unconditional pretraining first learns amino acid combinations and sequence patterns. Its weights remain fixed in downstream tasks, continually supplying a general generative prior.

Residual model: Learn to follow the target condition

It reads the current state, reference sequence, and target property, then learns the additional conditional correction that guides candidates toward the target.

For discrete protein sequences, the two components are combined at the level of token logits:

\[\ell_{\mathrm{cond}} =\operatorname{stopgrad}(\ell_{\mathrm{prior}})+r_\phi.\]

The training loss applies to the combined prediction and updates only the residual model. The continuous score decomposition is a design motivation; the implemented residual does not directly compute a classifier gradient.

The combined prediction then follows the update rule of its generative framework: BFN updates probability beliefs over amino acid classes, while DFM updates a discrete token sequence. Both reuse a fixed sequence prior, with the residual handling conditional adaptation.

From diffusion to discrete sequence generation

04 Understand probability beliefs and discrete jumps through gradual denoising

BFN: Accumulate class information through Bayesian updates

The state \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is a probability belief over \(K\) amino acid classes, initialized to a uniform distribution. The network reads these probabilities and predicts a clean-token distribution \(q\).

\[m\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(a(K e_x-\mathbf{1}),\,aK I\right).\]
\[\theta'_k=\frac{\theta_k\exp(m_k)}{\sum_j\theta_j\exp(m_j)}.\]

\(e_x\) is the token's one-hot encoding, and \(a>0\) controls the information added in this step. Training constructs a noisy message \(m\) from the true token. During generation, a predicted token is sampled from \(q\), a message is constructed, and the belief is updated. The update follows Bayes' rule, and the network continues to use the full sequence context at the next step.

Rather than directly updating a noisy token, BFN updates continuously valued class probabilities.

DFM: Update discrete tokens using transition rates

DFM-abs starts from an entirely masked sequence; DFM-uni starts from uniformly random tokens. Taking the linear masking path of DFM-abs as an example, \(t\) runs from 0 to 1, and an individual position gradually changes from mask \(M\) to clean token \(x\):

\[p_t(z\mid x)=(1-t)\delta_M(z)+t\delta_x(z).\]
\[R_t(M\!\to\!k)=\frac{q(k\mid\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}{1-t},\qquad t<1.\]

\(\delta\) denotes a distribution concentrated on the specified token, \(k\) is an amino acid class, and \(q\) predicts the clean token. Over a small interval \(\Delta t\), a masked position jumps to \(k\) with probability approximately \(R_t\Delta t\). In the base generation process without a corrector, positions that have been revealed remain unchanged.

DFM constructs discrete transitions from the clean-token posterior, moving the probability distribution along the chosen path from noise toward a sequence.

The residual changes the prediction; the generative framework determines the update

Both can use \(q=\operatorname{softmax}(\ell_{\mathrm{prior}}+r_\phi)\) as the clean-token prediction. BFN uses it to generate messages and update beliefs; DFM uses it to construct transition rates. The conditional residual in the preceding section therefore retains the same division of work.

A general prior for sequence generation

05 Check that the base model is learning before training the conditional residual

The residual approach depends on the base generator supplying a usable prior. Pretraining first learns generative patterns in natural protein sequences; the downstream stage then freezes the prior and trains a correction for the property condition.

Both approaches show periods of declining loss: BFN still fluctuates later in training, while masking-path DFM continues to improve more gradually. These histories support exploring downstream methods, but do not establish that the base models are fully trained.

Using such a prior for residual prediction lets the task network draw on learned sequence patterns, while also exposing it to limits in the prior's coverage. Further analysis needs to distinguish whether weaknesses in conditional control arise from the base generation capability, residual capacity, or the training data and evaluation protocol.

Evaluate loss reduction and property control separately

Pretraining loss assesses how well the base model learns the generative objective; downstream experiments assess whether candidates approach the target property. Improvement in the former cannot substitute for the latter, nor establish that new sequences have the desired function in real experiments.

Residual prediction · Performance relative to the baseline

06 Which property tasks benefit from the residual approach?

The experiments compare residual approaches for BFN and masking-path DFM against their respective single-network baselines for direct conditional prediction. The magnitude of the changes varies across tasks, and improvements must be read alongside evaluation coverage and editing behavior.

The baseline already uses protein language model initialization, but does not load this project's generative pretraining weights. The residual approach loads and freezes the generative prior, then trains the conditional residual. This comparison measures the full approach; it does not isolate the individual contributions of generative pretraining, model capacity, and residual parameterization.

Method recap

07 Explicitly separate the sequence prior and conditional correction to improve property control

The residual approach explicitly separates the sequence prior from the conditional correction and improves variant-effect control over the corresponding baselines. DFM’s improvement is more pronounced on the binding-property task.

Understand the division of work through score decomposition

\[s_{\mathrm{c}}=s_{\mathrm{u}}+\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(y\mid x_t).\]

The unconditional term supplies the language of protein sequences, while the conditional term supplies the direction toward the target property. One model learns which sequences are more plausible; the other learns how to follow the target condition.

Implement this intuition with a residual for discrete sequences

\[\ell_{\mathrm{cond}}=\operatorname{stopgrad}(\ell_{\mathrm{prior}})+r_\phi.\]

The base model learns sequence patterns in advance and remains frozen; the residual model focuses on learning the conditional correction. Together, they predict amino acids at each step, letting BFN and DFM reuse the learned sequence language for targeted generation.
